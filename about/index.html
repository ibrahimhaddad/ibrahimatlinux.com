About

I scale engineering organizations and open source ecosystems

Twenty-five years across both. The Linux Foundation, the PyTorch Foundation, Samsung, and now the software platform inside Volvo's cars.

Those two things look unrelated from the outside. In practice they are the same problem approached from opposite ends, and I have spent my career moving between them.

At the Linux Foundation, as Executive Director of LF AI & Data, I took a foundation with 9 members and 3 projects and grew it into one of the most influential open source AI organizations in the world: membership up eightfold to 77 companies including AWS, Microsoft, IBM, and Nvidia, projects from 3 to 70, and a developer community of more than 100,000 people across 3,000 organizations. I co-created the Model Openness Framework, now an industry standard for AI model transparency, and founded the Generative AI Commons, which brought more than 80 organizations under one neutral governance model for open source generative AI.

Concurrently I was Founding Executive Director of the PyTorch Foundation. I assembled the initial coalition across AMD, AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Nvidia, doubled the foundation's revenue inside a year, and grew the contributor base by 36% in six months.

Before the Linux Foundation I built Samsung's global open source engineering organization from nothing to more than 100 engineers across three continents. I set open source strategy company-wide, embedded it into product delivery, and led open source technical due diligence for Samsung NEXT. I also co-founded the Open Connectivity Foundation and served as its elected Vice President.

Today I lead the Infotainment Engineering organization at Volvo Cars, overseeing the software teams delivering the next-generation Android-based in-vehicle infotainment platform across multiple vehicle programs. That includes the Volvo EX60, the first fully electric Volvo designed, developed, and built in Sweden, with Google Gemini integrated as a core AI capability.

Along the way I have advised HP, Samsung, Huawei, Ericsson, DocuSign, and the World Bank Group, presented on AI regulation to the European and UK Parliaments, and served on technical and academic advisory boards in North America and Europe. I have written or co-written 7 books, 18 e-books, and more than 150 technical reports on open source strategy, AI governance, and license compliance. I hold a Ph.D. with honors in Computer Science from Concordia University.

Dual US and Canadian citizen. I work in English, French, and Arabic, and have built teams across North America, Europe, and Asia.

You cannot govern software well without having shipped it.

And you cannot ship at scale on software you have no standing to influence. Very few people have sat in both seats: the engineering organization that ships the product, and the neutral foundation that governs the software underneath it.

I have spent twenty-five years moving between the two. The view from each one changes how you use the other.

Formal bio

Ibrahim Haddad, Ph.D., is a technology executive who bridges open source ecosystem leadership with enterprise-scale engineering delivery. Across twenty-five years he has built and scaled globally distributed engineering organizations, founded two industry foundations, and advised Fortune 500 companies, technology leaders, foundations, and government bodies on open source strategy, governance, compliance, and AI policy.

As Executive Director of LF AI & Data at the Linux Foundation, he grew the foundation from 9 members and 3 projects into one of the world's most influential open source AI organizations, expanding membership eightfold to 77 companies, scaling from 3 to 70 projects, and building a developer community of more than 100,000 across 3,000 organizations. He co-created the Model Openness Framework, an industry standard for AI model transparency, and founded the Generative AI Commons, bringing more than 80 organizations under neutral governance for open source generative AI. Concurrently he served as Founding Executive Director of the PyTorch Foundation, where he assembled the initial coalition across AMD, AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Nvidia, doubled revenue inside a year, and grew the contributor base by 36% in six months.

At Samsung, as Distinguished Engineer and VP of R&D, he built the company's global open source engineering organization from nothing to more than 100 engineers across three continents, defined and executed open source strategy company-wide, and led open source technical due diligence for Samsung NEXT. He also co-founded the Open Connectivity Foundation and served as its elected Vice President.

He is a recognized expert in open source license compliance and OSPO development, has advised HP, Samsung, Huawei, Ericsson, DocuSign, and the World Bank Group, and has presented on AI regulation to the European and UK Parliaments. He has served on technical and academic advisory boards across North America and Europe. He is the author or co-author of 7 books, 18 e-books, and more than 150 technical reports, and holds a Ph.D. with honors in Computer Science from Concordia University, where he was awarded the J. W. McConnell Memorial Graduate Fellowship and the Concordia University 25th Anniversary Fellowship.

He is currently Head of Infotainment Engineering at Volvo Cars, leading the software teams delivering the next-generation Android-based in-vehicle infotainment platform. A dual US and Canadian citizen, he works in English, French, and Arabic.
