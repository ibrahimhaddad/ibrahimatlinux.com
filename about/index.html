About

Engineering leadership and open source strategy

Twenty-five years of building engineering organizations inside some of the largest technology companies in the world, and founding two of the open source ecosystem's consequential foundations. Still doing both.

I have spent my career in two places that rarely overlap. Inside companies, building the engineering organizations that ship software to millions of people. And outside them, in the neutral ground of foundations, where competitors agree on how the software underneath everyone's products is going to be governed.

At Samsung I founded the Open Source Group and grew it to more than 100 engineers across seven countries. I had no line authority over any of the business units I needed to change, which turned out to be the useful part of the job: you learn quickly that a policy nobody asked for only survives if the engineers it lands on can see what it buys them.

At the Linux Foundation I grew LF AI & Data into the largest neutral open source AI ecosystem, and served concurrently as Founding Executive Director of the PyTorch Foundation, where the work was getting AMD, AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and NVIDIA to agree on one governance model for software they all depend on and none of them controls. I co-created the Model Openness Framework, now a reference standard for AI model transparency, and launched the Generative AI Commons.

I have advised the World Bank Group, and presented on AI regulation to the European and UK Parliaments. I have written 7 books and 19 e-books, and more than 150 publications. I hold a Ph.D. with honors in Computer Science from Concordia University. Today I am Head of Infotainment Engineering at Volvo Cars, which puts me back on the delivery side of the problem, shipping an in-vehicle software platform across three platforms and two hardware architectures.

I have run both sides of open source.

The engineering organization that ships the product, and the neutral foundation that governs the software underneath it. Very few people have sat in both seats, and the ones who have tend to make better calls in either.

You cannot govern software well without having shipped it, and you cannot ship at scale on software you have no standing to influence.

Formal bio

Ibrahim Haddad, Ph.D., is a technology executive who bridges open source ecosystem leadership with enterprise-scale engineering delivery. Across twenty-five years he has built and scaled globally distributed engineering organizations, founded two industry foundations, and advised Fortune 500 companies, technology leaders, foundations, and government bodies on open source strategy, governance, compliance, and AI policy.

As Vice President of AI Strategic Programs at the Linux Foundation, he grew LF AI & Data into the largest neutral open source AI ecosystem. Concurrently, he served as Founding Executive Director of the PyTorch Foundation. He co-created the Model Openness Framework, now a reference standard for AI model transparency, and launched the Generative AI Commons to advance secure, ethical, and accessible AI innovation. At Samsung, as Distinguished Engineer and VP of R&D, he founded and scaled the company's global Open Source Group to over 100 engineers across seven countries, embedding open source strategy and compliance across business units without formal line authority. He also co-founded the Open Connectivity Foundation and served as its elected Vice President.

He is a recognized expert in open source license compliance and OSPO development, has advised the World Bank Group, and has presented on AI regulation to the European and UK Parliaments. A staunch advocate for collaborative development, he believes open source delivers faster, better, and more cost-effective innovation. He is the author of 7 books, 19 e-books, and over 150 publications, and holds a Ph.D. with honors in Computer Science from Concordia University, where he was awarded the J. W. McConnell Memorial Graduate Fellowship and the Concordia University 25th Anniversary Fellowship.

Over his career he has held executive and technical leadership roles at Volvo Cars, Samsung, the Linux Foundation, Ericsson, Motorola, Palm, and Hewlett-Packard, delivering complex software platforms across telecommunications, mobile computing, automotive, and AI. He is currently Head of Infotainment Engineering at Volvo Cars.
